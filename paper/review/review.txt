Here's a curated list — grouped by why you'd read each one:

Closest analogs — read these as templates for your paper's structure and validation section

1. Kehl et al., "Open-source lab hardware: Driver and temperature controller for high compliance voltage, fiber-coupled butterfly lasers," HardwareX 10 (2021) e00240 (https://www.hardware-x.com/article/S2468-0672(21)00070-5/fulltext) — the closest in spirit to Koi: a precision analog current driver + measurement for a photonics application, from a NASA JPL group, ~$400 build. Look at how they structure the characterization (current accuracy, noise, temperature stability) and the level of detail in build instructions. This is the quality bar.
2. MYSTAT: A compact potentiostat/galvanostat for general electrochemistry measurements, HardwareX 9 (2021) (https://www.hardware-x.com/article/S2468-0672(20)30072-9/fulltext) — effectively a low-cost single-channel SMU (±200 mA, ±12 V, source and measure). Its validation section (accuracy vs a commercial instrument, noise, linearity) is the same problem you have, solved acceptably for reviewers. Also citable as prior open source-measure hardware. GitHub repo here (https://github.com/matthew-yates/MYSTAT) — note how the released files map to their Design Files table.

Multichannel architecture examples — cite as prior open multichannel drivers

3. Multichannel pulse high-current driver of magnetic actuator, HardwareX 11 (2022) (https://www.hardware-x.com/article/S2468-0672(22)00031-1/fulltext) — a 4-channel current driver; useful both as a citation ("existing open multichannel drivers target different regimes") and to see how a multichannel-driver paper handles per-channel documentation without repeating itself 4×.
4. Modular multi-channel high voltage arbitrary waveform generator, HardwareX (2024) (https://www.hardware-x.com/article/S2468-0672(24)00020-8/fulltext) — modular/stackable multichannel architecture like your controller + daughterboards. Worth reading for how they present a scalable system (base unit + N modules) in the BOM and build instructions — the same presentation problem you have with 1 controller + 8 daughterboards.
5. PassStat, a simple but fast, precise and versatile open source potentiostat, HardwareX (2022) (https://www.hardware-x.com/article/S2468-0672(22)00035-9/fulltext) — a minimal precision instrument paper; good example of a tight, short HardwareX paper if you want to see the lower bound of scope that still passes review.

Prior art for Section 1 (beyond HardwareX)

6. Multi-channel Precision Bias Source for Experiments on Quantum Dots (preprint) (https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202107.0458/v2/download) — 16-channel, 18-bit precision DC voltage source; the nearest thing to an open "Qontrol-class" multichannel source I found. Voltage-source rather than current-source-with-readback, which is exactly the differentiation argument for Koi.
7. Open hardware solutions in quantum technology, APL Quantum 1, 011501 (2024) (https://pubs.aip.org/aip/apq/article/1/1/011501/3267254/Open-hardware-solutions-in-quantum-technology) — a survey worth mining for further prior art (Sinara/ARTIQ ecosystem, QICK, NQontrol…). Your "sweep for open multichannel drivers before submission" todo can largely be satisfied by working through this paper's reference list.

Reading strategy: Kehl and MYSTAT for how to write yours (especially validation and build sections), items 3–4 for how to present multichannel/modular, items 6–7 for what to cite as the open-hardware landscape in Hardware in context. Notably, none of these combine dense multichannel current drive with per-channel voltage readback — which confirms the gap statement now in your Section 1.
